The problem

The hardest question is often not “Was it right?”

It is: Can we prove the information existed before anyone knew what was correct? AITP protects the order of evidence so hindsight cannot quietly rewrite the story.

01

Lock the target

The hidden answer is fixed first.

Cryptographic commitment
02

Anchor chronology

Formal trials record independent evidence that the commitment existed beforehand.

External timestamp / witness
03

Seal the response

The participant's response is frozen before candidates appear.

Artifact fingerprint
04

Lock judgment

The judge commits to a ranking before the correct target is revealed.

Blinded ranking
05

Reveal & audit

The system verifies integrity before results are eligible for analysis.

Fail-closed audit

Tangible outputs

What you get is a better record—not a stronger story.

AITP turns a claim into inspectable artifacts that survive hindsight: what was fixed, what was said, what was judged, what was revealed, and whether the protocol stayed valid.

01

Evidence packet

Commitment, sealed response, candidate release, locked ranking, reveal, and tamper-evident event history.

Who knew what, and when
02

Audit verdict

A plain-language validity state plus the exact integrity checks that passed or failed.

Valid, invalid, and why
03

Analysis-ready record

Only eligible trials move into exact scoring and condition comparison; invalid trials remain preserved rather than hidden.

Evidence before interpretation
04

Frozen prediction

Exact wording, success/failure criteria, timing, content fingerprint, and append-only adjudication after reality answers.

Where it fits
The method travels wherever hindsight matters.
Hidden-information research is the core. The same prospective claim-integrity discipline can support adjacent contexts where chronology, blinding, null models, and preregistration matter.

    

      
01
Hidden-target research
Test whether a participant identifies information unavailable through ordinary channels.

      
02
Expert intuition
Compare expert judgments against baseline or control conditions prospectively.

      
03
AI forecasting
Freeze model versions, outputs, scoring rules, and ground truth before results are known.

      
04
Prediction integrity
Preserve exact future-facing claims and adjudication rules before the outcome.

      
05
Anomaly qualification
Separate observation, conventional null models, confounders, and unexplained residuals.

      
06
Reproducibility
Preserve protocol state so independent teams can inspect and repeat the method.

    

  


  

    
The boundary
A test is not a conclusion.
An unusual result can justify further investigation. It does not, by itself, identify a source, mechanism, intelligence, or ontology.
H0 Ordinary explanationH1 Condition differenceH2 Anomalous information effectH3 Additional-agent modelH4 Specific source identity
